Impulse versus intention

Technology is here, and it's for everyone. If we don't use it, we fall behind.

However, we shouldn't let it replace our own judgment, our critical thinking, our curiosity.

In my first job, in 2003, I remembered every client's phone number. It just came to me. Since smartphones, I cannot remember anyone's. Those are simple little things, and we grew a lot. This revolution is a little different. We are using a tool for our thinking and reasoning.

So think first, then ask. Leading AI, not the other way round.

You are the watcher

When you watch a movie, you start feeling as if you are in the movie. But when you come out of the theater, it's gone. Your life is like this. You are watching your life play out all the time, but you are unaffected by that.

This is the witness, sakshi in Sanskrit. It is a concept in Advaita Vedanta, a school of Indian philosophy, and it runs through the Ashtavakra Gita, one of the sacred scriptures of Hinduism. It is the idea I base my life on.

I'm not saying that you stop using technology. I'm just saying that you should be aware, and respond to your impulse rather than just reacting to it.

The Useful Pause: Think First, Then Ask

How to use AI and technology to their full potential without outsourcing your thinking, attention or judgment. The risk is simple. If you ask AI first, you may begin with its framing of the problem, not your own. And over time that can quietly weaken curiosity, independent reasoning and judgment. The talk brings in new research, told honestly, and the witness, in plain words.
